## Four answers (2 sentences each)
**How do you know the motion is real?** We measure the object's position in every WISE image from 2010 to 2024 and fit a straight line; a real object moves steadily and lands on the line in all epochs, noise does not. For our three movers the measured speed agrees with Gaia and the literature to 99.0–99.9 %, and a blind search with no prior knowledge finds exactly those three and nothing false.

**Why not just use IRSA's tools?** IRSA's tools are excellent for downloading and measuring SPHEREx data, but none of them plays a time-lapse movie or joins SPHEREx with 14 years of WISE on one grid. SkyLapse does both in a browser, explains each object in Azerbaijani and English, and needs no install or astronomy software.

**What does the 99 % mean?** It is how closely our measured speed and direction across the sky match the reference value: 100 % minus the size of the difference as a percentage of the true motion. For Barnard's Star the difference is about 17 thousandths of an arcsecond per year out of 10.4 arcseconds per year.

**Can SPHEREx alone see these motions?** Barely: SPHEREx has about one year of data and 6.15″ pixels, so even WISE 0855 moves only about 1–2 pixels within the SPHEREx data. With WISE added the baseline is 14–16 years and the same objects move 66–146″ in our measurements, which is easy to see and measure.
